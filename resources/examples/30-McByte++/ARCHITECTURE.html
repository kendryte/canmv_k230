1What McByte++ is

McByte++ accompanies Training-Free Long-Term Multi-Object Tracking for Sports Video Analytics (Stanczyk, Yoon & Brémond, arXiv:2608.15688). It is a tracking-by-detection system: a detector finds people in each frame, and the tracker’s job is to decide which box in frame t is the same person as which box in frame t−1, and to keep that identity stable over time.

Its contribution is that it does this without training anything. It takes off-the-shelf components and combines three cues on top of a ByteTrack core:

  1. A mask cue — a segmentation mask is propagated per tracklet and used to disambiguate matches that box overlap alone cannot resolve.
  2. Conditional camera motion compensation (CMC) — global camera movement is estimated and subtracted from the predicted boxes, but only when the estimate looks trustworthy.
  3. Online long-term re-identification — when someone leaves the scene and returns, an appearance memory gives them their original ID back.

Upstream this runs on an H100 at 8–20 FPS, using YOLOX-X for detection, EdgeTAM for masks, and OSNet for re-ID.

What this port is, honestly

Read this first

The algorithm is ported faithfully. The three neural networks it depends on are not, because they cannot fit on this chip. This is the McByte++ method running on a K230 — not a reproduction of its published accuracy. Do not expect SoccerNet numbers from it.

2The K230 hardware uses

Four independent blocks/models are in play, and understanding which does what explains most of the design.

Two properties of this arrangement matter:

  • chn0 never touches Python. It is bound to the video layer in hardware, so the live picture keeps painting even if the Python loop stalls or dies. This is a genuine debugging hazard: a frozen program still looks like it is running.
  • chn2 is 640×360, deliberately. The model input is 640×640, and the letterbox scales 640×360 by 1.0 and pads to 640. Feeding 1280×720 instead produces a byte-identical model input after ai2d’s 2× downscale — at 4× the frame buffer (2.76 MB vs 691 KB). The smaller channel is free.

3One frame, end to end

This is main() in app.py, and it mirrors §3.4 of the paper.

  1. Acquire

    snapshot() on chn2 gives the detector frame; to_numpy_ref() exposes it as a CHW uint8 array without copying. chn1 gives the aux frame as a normal image.Image.

    Either may fail: the firmware returns the integer -1 rather than a frame. Both are checked, and a miss skips the frame rather than propagating downstream.

  2. Detect

    ai2d letterboxes 640×360 into 640×640 (pad 140 rows top and bottom) and hands the result to the KPU. The model emits three feature maps, one per stride (8/16/32); aicube.anchorbasedet_post_process decodes anchors and runs NMS, returning [label, score, x1, y1, x2, y2].

    Output: a list of [x1, y1, x2, y2, score] in detector-frame coordinates.

  3. Predict

    Every tracklet advances one step through a constant-velocity Kalman filter. State is 8-dimensional — (x, y, a, h, vx, vy, va, vh) — box centre, aspect ratio, height, and their velocities. Process and measurement noise both scale with the target’s height, exactly as ByteTrack does, so distant (small) targets are trusted more tightly than near ones.

    Lost tracklets have their height velocity zeroed first: we do not know they are still moving, and letting them coast unbounded is how a box ends up a thousand pixels off-screen.

  4. Compensate for camera motion

    If the camera panned, every box moved even though nobody walked. CMC estimates that global shift and applies it to the predictions before matching.

    The estimate comes from phase correlation on a 128×128 grayscale reduction of the aux frame against the previous frame’s. The paper uses ORB features and an affine fit; no ORB exists on this firmware, but phase correlation returns translation directly plus a correlation-peak response that makes an excellent reliability gate — which is precisely the “conditional” half of the method.

    An estimate is accepted only if:

    • the correlation response clears cmc_min_response (0.25),
    • the implied shift is under 25% of the frame in each axis.

    Rejected estimates do not overwrite the retained one; the last good estimate is reused for cmc_hold_frames and then abandoned. Pan and tilt are compensated; roll and zoom are not, unless cmc_similarity is enabled (log-polar, off by default because its response is weak on real scenes).

    Afterwards every state is clamped to plausible bounds. This is not in the paper — it is defence against a coasting tracklet producing geometry that later becomes an out-of-range array index or an off-screen draw call.

  5. Build the cost matrix

    cost[i][j] = 1 − IoU(track_i, detection_j). Nothing more; appearance never enters frame-to-frame matching, by design.

  6. Apply the mask cue

    Each tracklet carries a mask. Against each candidate box, two pixel-count ratios are formed:

    mc = |mask_i ∩ bbox_j| / |mask_i|     coverage — a gate
mf = |mask_i ∩ bbox_j| / |bbox_j|     fill — the discriminative term

    and where the pair is ambiguous (several near-equal costs in that row or column) or isolated (cost already above the match threshold), the cost is lowered: 

    cost[i][j] = cost[i][j] − mf      if mf ≥ 0.05 and mc ≥ 0.90

    The asymmetry is intentional: mc only gates, because several masks can sit inside one box and score identically; mf is what actually perturbs the cost.

    How the mask is produced here

    EdgeTAM is a transformer and will not fit beside the detector. Instead each tracklet keeps a luminance band — mean ± 2.5·MAD over the centre 60% of its matched box, EMA-updated — and the mask is the set of cells inside its predicted box whose luminance falls in that band, evaluated on an 80×48 mean-pooled grid.

    It is genuinely propagated: the box comes from the Kalman prediction after CMC, and the band is temporal state. But it is a proxy, not a segmentation.

    Intersections are answered in O(1) using a summed-area table built per tracklet per frame, so the cost of testing a mask against 20 detections does not scale with mask area.

  7. Assign

    Hungarian assignment via Jonker–Volgenant shortest augmenting paths, in plain Python lists (track counts are tens; the O(n³) term is negligible). Pairs above the threshold are priced out of the solver and filtered again afterwards on their original cost, mimicking lapjv’s cost_limit.

  8. The BYTE second pass, and the lifecycle

    This is what makes ByteTrack work. Detections are split by score:

    • high (≥ track_thresh) match against all tracklets, at cost ≤ 0.8;
    • low (≥ conf_low, < track_thresh) then match against whatever is still unmatched, at the stricter cost ≤ 0.5.

    The insight is that a weak detection in the right place is usually a real person who is partly occluded — far better evidence than discarding them and declaring the tracklet lost. Unmatched tracklets go lost and survive track_buffer (30) frames on prediction alone before being removed.

    Unmatched high detections are offered to unconfirmed tracklets (born last frame, not yet corroborated) and otherwise born as new tracklets, if they clear new_track_thresh. A newborn is not reported until it survives a second association — one-frame detector noise never reaches the output.

  9. Long-term re-ID

    Re-ID is deliberately not part of frame-to-frame matching. It fires in exactly two places:

    • On every reliable match, a descriptor is extracted, ℓ2-normalised, and folded into the tracklet’s running average (which is re-normalised).
    • When a tracklet dies, that average is deposited in a long-term identity memory alongside the ID it held.
    • When a tracklet is born, the memory is queried. A match requires mutual best agreement — the newcomer’s top candidate must rank the newcomer as its top candidate — and cosine similarity ≥ 0.8. On a match the newcomer inherits the old ID and the memory entry is deleted so it cannot be handed out twice. Otherwise the newcomer stays provisional for reid_finalize_window frames, re-querying each frame, before being confirmed as a genuinely new identity.

    The descriptor here

    The crop is resampled to an 8×16 thumbnail and read out as a 384-dim vector, then mean-centred before normalisation. The centring is essential: raw colour vectors sit at cosine ≈ 0.99 to each other, so the paper’s 0.8 threshold would accept everything. After centring, cosine similarity is the Pearson correlation of the two layouts, and 0.8 is a meaningful bar again.

    This keys on clothing colour and its vertical arrangement. It re-identifies people in different-coloured clothes well and teammates in identical kit badly — which is exactly the case OSNet exists to handle.

  10. Draw

    The overlay is drawn into an ARGB8888 OSD image and pushed to an OSD layer, which the VO blends over the live video. Per track: a coloured box (thicker if its identity was reclaimed), an ID label, and a fading ground-contact trail. A status bar carries FPS, track count, re-ID hits and CMC engagement.

    Every dimension derives from panel height, so the same code is legible on an 800×480 LCD and a 1080p monitor. Every draw call is clipped to the panel first — see §7.

4Coordinate spaces

Four spaces coexist. Confusing them is the easiest way to introduce a subtle bug, so they are worth stating plainly:

SpaceSizeUsed by
Detector frame640×360 all tracking: detections, Kalman state, IoU, MOT output
Aux frame320×192 CMC input, re-ID crops
Mask grid80×48 mask cue only (aux ÷ mask_grid_div)
Panel800×480 drawing only

Detector-frame is the canonical space. Nothing else is ever stored in a track. Conversions happen at the boundary and nowhere else: MaskPropagator holds sx/sy into grid cells, reid.extract scales into aux pixels, and Renderer scales into panel pixels.

Because all three channels come from one capture, they share a field of view — so a normalised position means the same thing in all four spaces, and boxes stay aligned with the video even though the panel is not 16:9. The LCD is 5:3 against a 16:9 sensor, so the picture is squeezed ~6% horizontally rather than cropped.

The port deliberately does not import /sdcard/libs (the stock PipeLine/AIBase/Ai2d helpers) and talks to nncase_runtime directly. The only file it needs off the card is the kmodel.

Why the detection thresholds moved

The paper’s 0.6 split and 0.7 birth threshold are calibrated to YOLOX-X, which scores high on sports footage. The stock CanMV demo for this kmodel runs at a confidence threshold of 0.2 — the same person scores far lower. Keeping 0.6/0.7 means nothing is ever confident enough to start a tracklet, and the tracker reports zero tracks forever. Set paper_thresholds = True to restore the published values if you swap in a detector calibrated like YOLOX’s.

cmc_size is not a style preference: find_displacement on a non-power-of-two input returns a zero displacement at zero response, silently, instead of an error.

7Failure modes this implementation has observed

These were all hit during development and are worth knowing before you modify anything.

A silent freeze is not a Python bug

If the board stops with no traceback, no Python exception occurred — the firmware aborted underneath. Look at resources (sensor channels, buffer pools, the nncase pool) and at native calls given out-of-range arguments, not at tracking logic.

Out-of-range draw calls are the classic way to trigger one. The firmware draw primitives are not guaranteed to clip. A person at the edge of frame produces a box with negative origin; a coasting lost tracklet produces one entirely off-screen. Both are clipped before any draw call, and text is drawn only when its whole extent lands on the panel.

image.Image is a factory function, not a class. isinstance(x, image.Image) raises TypeError rather than returning False. Test sensor.snapshot() failure against the integer -1.

The video layer lies to you. It is bound in hardware, so the LCD shows live camera output whether or not the Python loop is executing. A completely stalled program looks like a working one. Per-frame logging (cfg.debug = 1) is the only reliable way to know a frame completed.

Always tear down inside finally, including setup. A run that fails partway through Pipeline.create() leaves the sensor and display initialised, and the next run hangs in Display.init() until the board is power-cycled.

Never auto-run unverified code as /sdcard/main.py

If it takes down the firmware, the board wedges before USB debug comes up and every power cycle repeats it — recoverable only with a card reader. main.py therefore has a boot guard: a five-second countdown before touching hardware, and a /sdcard/mcbytepp.off flag file that skips the app entirely.

9Where this will and will not work

Reasonable expectations, given the substitutions:

  • Good: a handful of people, moderately separated, distinguishable clothing, a camera that pans rather than rolls or zooms.
  • Poor: crowds with heavy mutual occlusion; people in identical uniforms (the descriptor cannot separate them and re-ID will merge identities); very small or distant subjects (detector limit); fast zoom (uncompensated).
  • Degrades safely: if the mask cue or CMC cannot get a signal, both become inert and the system falls back to plain ByteTrack, which still tracks.

Each cue can be ablated independently — mask_enable, cmc_enable, reid_enable — which is the fastest way to find out what is actually earning its keep on your footage.
